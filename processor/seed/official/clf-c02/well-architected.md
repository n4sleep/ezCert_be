# AWS Well-Architected Framework

## What is the Well-Architected Framework

The AWS Well-Architected Framework helps cloud architects build secure, high-performing, resilient, and efficient infrastructure for their applications. It provides a consistent approach to evaluating architectures and implementing designs that scale over time. The framework is organized around six pillars.

## The six pillars

Operational excellence focuses on running and monitoring systems to deliver business value, and on continually improving processes and procedures. Security focuses on protecting information, systems, and assets while delivering business value through risk assessments and mitigation strategies, including identity and access management, detective controls, and data protection. Reliability focuses on workloads performing their intended functions correctly and consistently, including recovery from failures and meeting demands over time. Performance efficiency focuses on using computing resources efficiently to meet requirements while maintaining cost effectiveness as technology evolves. Cost optimization focuses on avoiding unnecessary costs, such as by selecting the right resource types and monitoring usage. Sustainability focuses on minimizing the environmental impacts of running cloud workloads, for example by optimizing utilization and designing for efficiency.

## Design principles

The framework includes design principles that apply across pillars, such as stop guessing capacity, automate wherever possible, enable traceability, and design for failure so that no single point of failure brings down the workload. Well-architected workloads use mechanisms that automatically respond to failure instead of reacting manually.

## AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)

The AWS Cloud Adoption Framework provides guidance for cloud adoption journeys. It organizes guidance into six perspectives: business, people, governance, platform, security, and operations. The business perspective ensures IT aligns with business requirements; the people perspective addresses training and organizational change; the governance perspective covers skills and processes to align IT strategy with business goals; the platform perspective covers the architecture of new solutions; the security perspective addresses security requirements; and the operations perspective covers ongoing operations of cloud workloads.
